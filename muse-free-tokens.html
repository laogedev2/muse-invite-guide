Is Muse Free? The 1-Billion-Token Invite, Explained Honestly

Short answer: Muse is Meta's personal AI agent. New users who join through an invite link and redeem the invite code in Settings within 48 hours of joining receive 1 billion Muse tokens each for inviter and invitee. The official invite page governs the actual reward and terms — read it before you count on anything.

Quick answers

Is Muse really free? Muse is free to join, but not unlimited free use forever. New users who redeem the invite code in Settings within 48 hours of joining receive a stated 1 billion tokens for both inviter and invitee. Token budgets, rate limits, and features still follow Muse's own terms.

Who can redeem the invite code? Only new users in the United States and Canada, and only within 48 hours of joining. If you are outside the supported regions, skip it and do not burn the code on an account that cannot use it. If you miss the window, the code cannot be redeemed retroactively.

What should I do first after joining? Redeem first, explore later. Open Settings, find the invite code entry, and redeem within 48 hours of joining, before the entry disappears. Then check the official invite page for the actual reward and terms, since figures here remain subject to it.

What the invite gives you

  • Join link: muse.ai/join
  • Invite code: BMIFSK — redeem in Settings within 48 hours of joining.
  • Stated reward: 1 billion Muse tokens for both sides (the inviter's stated figure is 10亿, and 10亿 = 1 billion; actual reward and validity per the official invite/redemption page).

What it does not mean

  • It is not a promise of unlimited free use forever. Token budgets, rate limits, and feature availability follow Muse's own terms.
  • It does not make Muse available everywhere: availability is US/Canada at the time of writing. If you are not in the US or Canada, skip it — do not burn the code on an account that cannot use it.
  • If you miss the 48-hour window, the code cannot be redeemed retroactively. Redeem first, explore later.

Who should take it

People who actually have recurring busywork — registrations, forms, research, drafting, follow-ups — and want an agent that does the work rather than explains it. See Who Is Muse For? (And Who Should Skip It) and Muse vs ChatGPT vs Claude (2026).

Who should skip it

If you only need occasional chat answers, a chatbot is cheaper and simpler. If you are outside the supported regions, wait. If you will not redeem within 48 hours, do not start the clock.
