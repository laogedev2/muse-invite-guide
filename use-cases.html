30 Things I Actually Delegate to My Personal AI Agent

Most AI assistants stop at advice. Muse — Meta's personal AI agent — is different in one specific way: it has its own computer, browser, and filesystem, it can connect to your email, calendar, and accounts, it remembers your preferences, and it finishes the job before it reports back. Below are the 30 things I hand it most often, each with the exact sentence I use to ask. Steal them verbatim.

Jump to: Everyday assistance · Work & communication · Content creation · Development & data · Life services

Everyday assistance

1. A morning brief that runs itself

Ask: "Every morning at 8, send me my schedule for the day and the three things that matter most."

Scheduled jobs run in the background whether your app is open or your phone is off, and the result lands as a message with a push notification.

2. Inbox triage

Ask: "Go through today's email, flag the ones that actually need my reply, and give me a one-line summary of the rest."

With Gmail or Outlook connected, it reads first and acts second — you only see what deserves your attention.

3. Voice notes instead of typing

Ask: Just dictate it — "Remind me to call the client tomorrow at 3."

Voice input becomes text, and voice notes get transcribed, so a spoken thought turns into a scheduled task without you typing a word.

4. Price and change watches

Ask: "Watch this product and tell me the moment it drops below $500."

Watches check on a cadence and alert you with a link to act on — no more refreshing pages yourself.

5. Business calls, handled

Ask: "Call this restaurant and book a table for four tonight; if they're full, ask for the earliest opening."

It prepares a brief, confirms the details with you first, makes the call (US businesses), and reports the outcome.

6. Standing preferences

Ask: "Remember: I don't eat spicy food, I take aisle seats, and invoices always go in this folder."

Preferences go into long-term memory and apply by default from then on.

Work and communication

7. Meeting notes → owned action items

Ask: "Turn these meeting notes into decisions, owners, and deadlines — then draft the recap email."

Decisions and action items come out structured, with the follow-up email already written.

8. Draft-first email

Ask: "Draft an email to them covering these three points. Show me first — don't send until I approve."

Sending stays behind your approval; nothing goes out behind your back.

9. Cross-timezone scheduling

Ask: "Find a time next week when all four of us are free and send the invites in everyone's local time."

It reads calendar availability across timezones instead of playing email ping-pong.

10. Long documents, one page out

Ask: "Summarize this 30-page contract into one page, list the key numbers, and flag the risky clauses."

Documents and PDFs become decision-ready briefs.

11. Delegating from WhatsApp

Ask: Send it a WhatsApp message like "Check whether my flight tomorrow is delayed."

WhatsApp is a full working channel — you can assign and receive work there without opening another app.

12. Polite chasing

Ask: "They haven't replied to last week's quote. Draft a polite follow-up — don't send it yet."

It remembers the thread, drafts the nudge, and waits for your go-ahead.

Content creation

13. Article and post images

Ask: "Generate an image for this post: morning light, pour-over coffee by a window, warm tones."

Images come from Muse's image model and drop straight into your piece.

14. Short video clips

Ask: "Make a 10-second vertical clip: waves hitting rocks, upbeat electronic music."

Clips run about ten seconds with synthesized sound; ask for several and have them stitched for longer pieces.

15. Text to spoken audio

Ask: "Read this draft back to me as audio — I want to listen on the drive."

It generates spoken audio, including multi-voice podcast-style recordings.

16. Finished documents and decks

Ask: "Turn this data into an 8-slide deck, conclusion first, and export a PDF."

Real Word docs, PDFs, spreadsheets, and slide decks — saved to your Library, downloadable anytime.

17. Small interactive apps

Ask: "Build me a little expense-tracker web app that stores entries and totals them by month."

These are live apps with their own stored data, not screenshots of an idea.

18. A feed that follows your taste

Ask: "Less crypto in my Feed, more F1 and local gigs."

The Feed is a set of short editorial posts it writes for you on a schedule; one sentence re-aims it.

Development and data

19. Scripts that run, not snippets

Ask: "Write a script that sorts this folder of photos by the month they were taken — and run it."

It writes code on its own machine, executes it, and verifies the result.

20. Spreadsheet analysis

Ask: "Break this statement down by category and flag any unusually large charges."

It works directly on your files: totals, categories, anomalies.

21. Recurring page and data checks

Ask: "Check this page every morning and send me whatever changed."

Set it once; it keeps checking and reports only the deltas.

22. Parallel sub-agents

Ask: "Review these ten résumés in parallel and give me one ranked comparison."

Big independent batches get split across sub-agents and merged into a single answer.

23. Browser errands

Ask: "Log in to this site, download my last three monthly statements, and file them by month."

It drives a real browser — searches, forms, downloads — and comes back to you when a verification step needs a human.

24. Research with receipts

Ask: "Compare these three products for me — with sources — and end with a recommendation."

It searches the live web, reads the originals, and cites its sources so you can check the work.

Life services

25. End-to-end trip planning

Ask: "Plan five days in Tokyo next month: compare flights and hotels, and lay the itinerary out in one plan I can adjust."

Flights, stays, transport, and activities coordinated in a single living itinerary.

26. Restaurant reservations

Ask: "Does this place have a table for four on Saturday night? If yes, book it."

Through connected booking platforms, it checks real availability before reserving.

27. Shopping with a final say

Ask: "Compare the total price — tax and shipping included — for these headphones, then check with me before buying."

Every purchase needs your approval, every time.

28. Flight status tracking

Ask: "Track my flight and tell me immediately if it's delayed, cancelled, or changes gates."

Schedule and gate changes surface as messages instead of airport surprises.

29. Goals that keep score

Ask: "Set a goal: run a half marathon by year-end. Remind me to train three times a week and log my progress."

Goals live in their own tab with plans and a progress history it maintains for you.

30. Smart home control

Ask: "Dim the living room lights."

With Muse Home Link (an experimental bridge for paid plans), it can discover and control supported devices on your home network.
